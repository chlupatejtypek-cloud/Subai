# Curiosity Vault

> **Tagline:** *Everyday things. Extraordinary stories.*

Positioning: a faceless, English-language edutainment channel that tells the hidden history, surprising science, and strange true stories behind everyday objects, customs, mysteries, and "did you know" moments. Storyteller-meets-teacher energy: the viewer should finish every video feeling *"I never thought about it that way."*

---

## 1. Purpose

- Turn ordinary things and overlooked questions into genuinely interesting short documentaries (7–10 min).
- Reward curiosity and attention span — videos that feel like a friend telling you a fascinating story, not like a lecture or a slideshow.
- Build a durable, evergreen library: most videos stay relevant and searchable for years, so the channel compounds.

## 2. Audience

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Geography | Global, English-speaking (US/UK/CA/AU dominant) |
| Age | 18–44 core |
| Mindset | Curious browsers, "shower-thoughts" people, fans of channels like *Half as Interesting*, *Weird History*, *BE AMAZED*, short documentaries |
| Consumption | Mostly YouTube browse/suggested; often on mobile; many watch with sound |
| Secondary | English learners who enjoy clear, captioned narration |

## 3. Why this channel is a good fit for AI automation

- **Script-first production:** no presenter, no live recording, no schedules to coordinate.
- **Faceless:** visuals come from licensed/AI-generated images, archival/public-domain footage, and simple motion — no expensive shoots.
- **Evergreen topics:** the backlog does not rot, so automation can batch ideas, scripts, and voiceovers.
- **Clear quality bar:** facts + story + pacing are measurable and reviewable before publishing.

## 4. Format & length

- **Target length:** 7–10 minutes (sweet spot for retention and suggested videos).
- **Structure:** cold-open hook → promise → story beats (setup → twist → payoff) → quick recap → CTA.
- **Pace:** one idea per ~60–90 seconds; cut anything that doesn't serve the story.
- **Always:** English narration with hard subtitles (captions styled), chapters in the description, a clean intro sting ≤ 4 s.
- **Script length reference:** ≈ 1,150–1,400 words for 7–9 minutes at a ~150–155 wpm narration pace.

## 5. Cadence

- **Ideal:** 2 videos per week (e.g. Tue + Fri, ~16:00 UTC).
- **Minimum to stay healthy:** 1 video per week.
- Consistency beats volume. If a slot slips, publish quality later rather than rushing garbage.

## 6. Content pillars

| Pillar | What it covers | Suggested share |
|---|---|---|
| **Untold Origins** | The hidden history of everyday things & customs: why we clink glasses, how the fork won Europe, the real story behind "ring around the rosie" | ~40 % |
| **Mini Mysteries** | One mystery per video — historical or everyday — resolved honestly, or explicitly "we still don't know" (that is a valid, honest ending) | ~25 % |
| **Fact Shock** | Surprising but fully verifiable facts and mini top-5s with real sources | ~20 % |
| **Time Capsules** | Short documentaries on bizarre historical places, events, and vanished worlds | ~15 % |

## 7. Topic recipe (how the 5 ideas are generated)

When the agent offers topics for this channel it follows the rules in **Step 1.3 of the README**, plus these channel-specific rules:

- **Mix the pillars:** five ideas must not come from one pillar (e.g. 2× Untold Origins, 1× Mini Mysteries, 1× Fact Shock, 1× Time Capsules).
- **Specific beats broad:** *"Why do we clink glasses?"* yes · *"History of drinking"* no.
- **The curiosity gap must be real:** the title raises a question the video actually answers.
- **Verifiable:** the core claim must be checkable in public sources within a few searches.
- **Evergreen default;** if web tools are available, at most 1–2 ideas may ride a current trend/news hook (labeled "trend").
- **Banned as topics:** unfounded conspiracy theories, fake "lost civilization" claims, supernatural stories sold as fact, graphic violence, and anything that would need graphic imagery or hateful framing.
- **Honest uncertainty:** mysteries are presented as mysteries — open questions are an ending, not clickbait.

## 8. Script structure template

| Beat | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0:00–0:45 | Open mid-story or with a question that creates the gap; show the payoff coming. No long channel intro. |
| **Promise** | ~0:45–1:15 | One sentence: what the viewer will know by the end. |
| **Setup** | 1:15–~30 % | The familiar thing, the ordinary version of the story — make the viewer feel "I know this". |
| **Turn** | middle | The twist/underneath layer: the detail that changes the picture. |
| **Payoff** | ~last 2 min | The satisfying resolution or the honest "we don't know — and that's the wonder". |
| **Recap + CTA** | last 20–30 s | 2–3 sentence recap; one specific comment question; "subscribe for the next one". |

Style notes: present tense where possible, concrete numbers/dates/names, one clear mental image per minute, no filler ("um", "in this video we will…"), no false drama.

## 9. Voice

- One consistent, warm, curious narrator voice, **en-US** (neutral American accent).
- Energetic but not shouty; a storyteller, slightly amused, never condescending.
- ~150–155 wpm; clear enunciation (also helps non-native English listeners).
- The same voice across videos = part of the channel's identity.

## 10. Visual identity

- **Palette:** deep navy (`#0B1B2B`) background, warm amber accent (`#FFB347`), off-white text — "vault / golden curiosity" mood.
- **Thumbnails:** consistent layout — one clear subject image, ≤ 5 words of text, the amber corner mark "CV", high contrast. No misleading thumbnails (the video must deliver what the thumbnail promises).
- **Footage:** licensed stock/archival or AI-generated stills + subtle motion (Ken Burns-style pans/zooms); public-domain archival for historical pieces; on-screen text for numbers and names.
- **Subtitles:** styled captions always on; keyword highlights in amber.
- **Intro/outro:** ≤ 4 s sting with the tagline *"Everyday things. Extraordinary stories."*; end screen with next-video teaser.

## 11. Packaging (used from Step 6 onward)

- **Titles:** English, title case, ≤ 60 characters, curiosity-driven, no ALL CAPS spam, no emoji in titles.
- **Thumbnails:** see §10.
- **Description:** 1–2 line hook → chapters with timestamps → 2–3 real source links → a short comment prompt. No keyword stuffing.
- **Tags:** only the handful that genuinely describe the video.

## 12. Quality checklist (every video, before it can be approved)

- [ ] Topic fits a pillar and the claim is verifiable with real sources.
- [ ] Hook answers the title's promise — no bait-and-switch.
- [ ] Script length matches the target duration; no filler paragraphs.
- [ ] Every factual claim is checked; uncertain claims are phrased honestly.
- [ ] English is correct (US spelling), narration script reads aloud naturally.
- [ ] No copyrighted music/footage without a license; assets are licensed/public-domain/AI-generated.
- [ ] Thumbnail + title + description are consistent and honest.
- [ ] Video passes YouTube policy sanity check (safe for general audience).

## 13. Rules: do / don't

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Lead with the story, teach on the way | Lecture, drone, or pad runtime |
| Verify claims and link sources | Invent facts, dates, or quotes |
| Respect that mysteries can stay open | Sell speculation as fact |
| Keep it safe for a general audience | Use graphic imagery or hateful framing |
| Use the consistent voice & visual identity | Change narrator/look every video |
| Stay in English (en-US) | Mix languages in titles/scripts |
| Deliver what the thumbnail promises | Clickbait that contradicts the video |

## 14. KPIs & iteration (used from Step 9 onward)

- Watch: **avg. view duration** and **retention curve** (are hooks/payoffs working?).
- Growth: **CTR** on impressions (is the packaging honest and clickable?).
- Library: which pillars pull their weight — rebalance the §6 shares after every ~10 videos.
- Feedback: the pinned comment question per video feeds the next topic batch.
