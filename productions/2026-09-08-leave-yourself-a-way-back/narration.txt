Before you switch tasks, leave yourself a way back. You're pulled into a meeting, but part of your attention is still stuck on the report you left unfinished. Researchers call this attention residue. It can make the new task harder, especially when you expect to return to the old one under time pressure. Research on interruptions found that briefly planning your return can help you focus on the task interrupting you. Try a restart note. Write where you stopped, the next concrete step, and when you'll return. For example: paragraph two needs evidence; find the survey results; return after the meeting. This isn't a way to make multitasking free. It's a way to stop carrying the unfinished task in your head while you do the next one.
