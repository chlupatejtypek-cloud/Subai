# Research before script — 2026-09-08
Topic: ready-to-resume planning, not smartphone checking. Practical angle: prepare your return so you can attend to the interrupting task now.

Primary peer-reviewed article abstract read at university repository:
https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/tasks-interrupted-how-anticipating-time-pressure-on-resumption-of/
Leroy & Glomb (2018), Organization Science 29(3),380–397; https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2017.1184
Four studies supported the prediction that anticipating time pressure when resuming interrupted work makes disengagement harder, increasing attention residue and harming performance on the interrupting task. Brief reflection and planning of one's return mitigated this effect. Full article methods/effect sizes not accessed; do not invent statistics, exact dose or universal benefit.

Corroborating reporting / author interview read:
https://mndaily.com/city/adproductivity/01/29/2018/snoadmin/
Reports four studies, one survey of202 employees; two intervention studies involved reflecting on where one left off and writing it down. Improved performance on interrupting task. This is reporting of the SAME paper, not an independent replication.

Boundaries: no claim that multitasking becomes costless, no guaranteed cure, no asserted percent productivity gain, no claim the paper demonstrated faster return. A report/paragraph/meeting example is our practical illustration, not verbatim experimental instructions. Use “can help,” retain anticipated time-pressure boundary. No statistical chart needed.
