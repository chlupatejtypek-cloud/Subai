A conversation can feel less friendly even when you don't clearly notice what's changed. In a study, people watched a group conversation and imagined being one of the speakers. Adding a four-second silence after her statement made viewers feel more rejected. Yet their estimates of the response delay didn't significantly differ from the smooth-conversation group. That's the useful twist: disrupted flow can change the feeling of connection, without proving anyone dislikes you. Four seconds isn't a universal awkwardness limit, and this was a specific experimental setup. So before apologizing, separate the observation from your interpretation: fact, they paused; guess, they're judging me. They might be choosing their words. Give them a moment, then ask, what do you think? Treat the pause as missing information, not a verdict.
