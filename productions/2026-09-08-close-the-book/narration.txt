Close the book. Can you explain what you just read? Rereading can feel productive because the page gets easier to follow. But recognizing an explanation isn't the same as recalling it without help. In experiments with students learning short passages, rereading helped on an immediate test. Practicing recall worked better when the test came days later. And the students who kept studying were more confident, despite remembering less after a week. So try this after a short section. Close the book and write the main ideas from memory. Then reopen it, check what you missed, and correct your notes. Later, try recalling the ideas again. The point isn't to struggle forever or memorize mistakes. It's to practice producing the knowledge, not just looking at it. A blank page can be a better learning tool than another comfortable reread.
