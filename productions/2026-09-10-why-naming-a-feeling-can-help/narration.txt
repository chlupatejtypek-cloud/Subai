Naming a feeling is supposed to calm you down. Here's the strange part: it worked, but not the way people expect. Researchers had spider-fearful volunteers face a real, live spider. One group said out loud what they felt. I'm anxious. That spider is disgusting. Others reframed the situation, or distracted themselves. A week later, with a different spider in a different room, the labeling group showed the smallest physical stress response. Yet when asked how afraid they felt, the groups reported about the same. So naming your fear didn't delete it. Your body settled first, and the feeling took longer to agree. And the more specific the words were, the bigger the effect. So next time your chest tightens before a call, don't tell yourself to relax. Say what's actually here. Nervous. Embarrassed. Your body may listen before you do.
