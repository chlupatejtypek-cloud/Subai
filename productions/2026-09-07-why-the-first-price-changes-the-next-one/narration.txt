That crossed-out price can make an ordinary deal feel amazing. But the surprising part is that an anchor doesn't even have to be a price. In a classic experiment, people saw a number from a wheel, compared it with the share of United Nations members that were African countries, then estimated that share. The group shown ten gave a median estimate of twenty-five percent; the group shown sixty-five gave forty-five percent. The wheel offered no useful evidence, yet estimates shifted toward it. That's anchoring: a starting number can pull your judgment. So before opening a sale page, write down what the item is worth to you and compare real alternatives. A big discount from someone else's starting price isn't the same as a good deal.
