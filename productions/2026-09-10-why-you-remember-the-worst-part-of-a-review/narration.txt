Your review was forty minutes long. Thirty-nine of them were fine. So why does the whole thing feel like a disaster? Researchers asked patients to rate their pain every single minute of an uncomfortable medical procedure. Some lasted four minutes. Some lasted sixty-nine. Then they asked how bad it was overall. Here's what predicted the answer. Not the length. Length had almost no relationship to the memory at all. What predicted it was the worst moment, and the last few minutes. Two points. Everything else got averaged away. So they tried something strange. In a trial with six hundred and eighty-two patients, half had the procedure extended by an extra minute that was uncomfortable, but gentler. More total discomfort. And those patients remembered it as better. They were also more likely to come back. Your memory is not keeping the receipts. It keeps the peak, and it keeps the ending. So when the criticism lands last, that becomes the whole review. Go back and read the entire thing before you decide what it meant.
