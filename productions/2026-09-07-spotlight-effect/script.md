# Why Everyone Seems to Notice Your Mistakes

NARRATOR: One awkward moment. Suddenly, the whole room feels like an audience.

NARRATOR: That's the spotlight effect: we can overestimate how much others notice us.

NARRATOR: In one study, people wearing an embarrassing shirt overestimated how many observers remembered its picture.

NARRATOR: Your own experience feels vivid. It's easy to assume everyone else sees it that way.

NARRATOR: But feeling watched isn't proof that everyone is watching.

NARRATOR: Next time, ask: what did I actually observe, and what am I guessing?

NARRATOR: Then return to the conversation. A feeling isn't a head count.
