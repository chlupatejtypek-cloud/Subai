One awkward moment. Suddenly, the whole room feels like an audience.
That's the spotlight effect: we can overestimate how much others notice us.
In one study, people wearing an embarrassing shirt overestimated how many observers remembered its picture.
Your own experience feels vivid. It's easy to assume everyone else sees it that way.
But feeling watched isn't proof that everyone is watching.
Next time, ask: what did I actually observe, and what am I guessing?
Then return to the conversation. A feeling isn't a head count.
