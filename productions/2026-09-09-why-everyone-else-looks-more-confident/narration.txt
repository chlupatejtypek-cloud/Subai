You leave a good conversation, then spend the walk home wondering if they found you boring. But your review and their review may be very different. Researchers call this the liking gap. After people talked, researchers compared how much each person thought they were liked with how much their partner reported liking them. On average, people underestimated it. One explanation is that you hear your own awkward internal commentary, while the other person experiences the actual conversation. Your brain brought a film critic to a coffee chat. That doesn't mean everyone secretly adores you, or that a smile proves friendship. It's an average pattern, not a verdict about one person. So after your next conversation, separate what happened from what you guessed. “I paused” is an observation. “They thought I was boring” is an interpretation. If you enjoyed talking, a simple “Nice chatting earlier” can leave room for another conversation. You don't need to pretend it went perfectly. Just don't confuse your harshest guess with their opinion.
