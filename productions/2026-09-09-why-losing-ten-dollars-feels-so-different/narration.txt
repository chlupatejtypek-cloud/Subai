Would you rather gain five hundred, or lose five hundred? Easy question, until both leave you with exactly the same money. Imagine you're given a thousand, then gain five hundred. Now imagine you're given two thousand, then lose five hundred. Both finish at fifteen hundred. But the starting point changes the story. In a classic study, people choosing between a sure outcome and a gamble made different choices when equivalent final outcomes were framed as gains or losses. This is reference dependence: we evaluate changes against a starting point, not just the total. Your calculator sees the same answer. Your brain has hired two different headline writers. That doesn't mean everyone reacts identically, or that losses always hurt exactly twice as much. Before judging a deal, write down the final amounts, the probabilities, and any real costs. Then compare those, not just the words gain and lose. A different headline isn't automatically a different deal.
