# Research — Why Procrastination Feels Like Relief

## Decision

The first psychology-channel test explains procrastination as short-term mood repair rather than reducing it to laziness or poor scheduling. It ends with a small, concrete implementation intention.

## Primary research

1. Fuschia Sirois and Timothy Pychyl (2013), *Procrastination and the Priority of Short-Term Mood Regulation: Consequences for Future Self* — procrastination often prioritizes immediate mood repair when a task feels aversive; the future self bears the cost.
   - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/spc3.12011
   - Open author manuscript: https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91793/
2. Wieber and Gollwitzer's implementation-intention work — a concrete if–then cue delegates initiation to a specified situation and can help with starting an aversive task.
   - Accessible researcher summary: https://www.psychologytoday.com/gb/blog/dont-delay/201001/implementation-intentions-facilitate-action-control
3. A 2026 randomized trial of Mental Contrasting with Implementation Intentions found improved willingness and more timely task initiation among 81 undergraduates. It supports an actionable but appropriately modest recommendation rather than claiming a cure.
   - https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691825014829

## Creative synthesis

- Hook: surprising reward reversal — procrastination rewards the brain with relief.
- Mechanism: aversive feeling → phone avoidance → immediate relief → learned repetition.
- Cost: present relief transfers stress to the future self.
- Action: identify the feeling, then attach one tiny first action to a specific cue.
- Educational disclaimer: this is general psychology education, not diagnosis or individualized treatment.
