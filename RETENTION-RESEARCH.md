# Research-informed retention playbook

Reviewed2026-09-08. Goal: useful, satisfying40–60s psychology stories that viewers want to finish and share. No scientific paper establishes a guaranteed viral formula, an optimal joke quota, a mandatory10-image rhythm or a universal retention threshold.40–60s and minimum10 used images are owner production requirements, not scientific findings.

## What the evidence supports — and does not

**Curiosity and learning.** Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath (2014), Neuron84,486–496, DOI10.1016/j.neuron.2014.08.060: the primary-study institutional abstract reports improved memory for high-curiosity trivia information and incidental material on immediate and one-day tests. This concerns learning in an experimental trivia task, not YouTube completion or virality. Reviewed institutional abstract, not a full methods replication. [3](https://ucdavis.pure.elsevier.com/en/publications/states-of-curiosity-modulate-hippocampus-dependent-learning-via-t)

Application hypothesis: make the viewer understand exactly what they do not yet know; show enough of a concrete situation to make the answer matter. Deliver intermediate answers. Do not turn this into “dopamine hacks,” a fixed14-second pause, or unsupported claims about attention spans.

**Short-form curiosity prompts.** Curious Shorts (CHI2025), DOI10.1145/3706598.3713951, describes a prototype using question-based prompts to explore follow-up videos; results include more question-guided exploration and learning benefits particularly from follow-ups. This is closer to short video behavior but tests an interactive interface, NOT a guaranteed within-video script structure or organic YouTube distribution. Reviewed available paper sections through retrieved text. [4](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713951)

Application hypothesis: a specific question can motivate interest in an explanation, but a standalone Short must fulfil its own promise. Never hide the answer behind “part2” as a retention trick.

**Coherence versus irrelevant entertainment.** Sundararajan & Adesope (2020), Educational Psychology Review32,707–734, DOI10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4, meta-analysis abstract reports that interesting but irrelevant seductive details can hinder learning, with effects moderated by design/methodology. This is not proof that humor is always harmful, nor a direct Shorts retention experiment. Abstract reviewed; no invented effect size. [1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Keep-it-Coherent:-A-Meta-Analysis-of-the-Seductive-Sundararajan-Adesope/95806b02220471a55d9d18ebdc24d55d91973fd6)

Application hypothesis: use an occasional content-linked joke or reaction; remove unrelated gags, decorative UI and visual competition. One focal thought at a time. Humor is optional and must survive a clarity/read-aloud check; no claim that one joke scientifically raises retention.

**What YouTube actually reports.** Official YouTube help defines engaged views as staying beyond initial seconds excluding loops, stayed-to-watch as viewed vs swiped away, and Shorts average duration/percentage as based on engaged views and corresponding watch time. These metrics are not interchangeable; average percentage viewed is not identical to the percentage of people who finished. [5](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en-ie&ref_topic=12221641&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)

Official Search & Discovery help (fetched directly2026-09-08, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?hl=en) describes choosing to watch, duration/percentage watched, likes and satisfaction surveys, alongside personalization, topic interest, competition and seasonality. Good metrics alone do not guarantee reach. This fetched page was the Video help variant; do not present it as a precise disclosure of Shorts ranking weights.

Application: measure early choice to stay separately from how long engaged viewers watched. Inspect drops alongside narration/storyboard, then change one meaningful thing in future videos. Raw views alone do not diagnose script quality. Do not repeat third-party “80% means viral” rules as platform facts.

## Editorial rules to test, not pretend are proven laws
- Hook = specific observable oddity + an honest question. Bad: “This brain trick changes everything.” Better structure: familiar situation, unexpected consequence, what explains it? Verify that consequence before using it.
- Progress = new example, clue, mechanism, boundary or practical action. Rephrasing the same promise is not progress.
- Curiosity = one clear unresolved main question, not five unexplained jargon terms. Answer smaller questions throughout; give the main payoff before the final sentence, then show why it matters.
- Surprise must come from true evidence or a clearly hypothetical demonstration, never distorted study results.
- Humor: dry, brief, relevant, occasional; omit when forced. Stiles can react with mild disbelief without ridiculing the audience. No recycled catchphrase every video.
- Shareability: a useful observation viewers recognize in themselves, a clear demonstration, or advice they could send to one specific person. No fabricated outrage or universal diagnoses.
- Pacing: vary idea/visual rhythm without overwhelming viewers.10–15 meaningful illustrations over40–60s can support coherence; no rule that every few seconds needs a gimmick.
- Ending: resolve the question and leave a practical thought, not a long subscribe speech. A natural callback can reward rewatching; never design a confusing accidental loop that withholds closure.

## Required research/script artifacts
`research.md`:3 angle comparisons, semantic overlap check, primary/corroborating sources, claim-evidence table, uncertainty and viewer payoff.
`story-beats.md`: time targets, evolving viewer question, delivered information, visual, claim source, optional joke and reason it helps.
`script-review.md`: evidence fidelity, specificity of hook, payoff completeness, read-aloud clarity, no padding, optional humor decision, predicted weak beat to inspect after publication.
`performance-notes.md`: actual available metrics/time window/sample size, retention diagnosis and ONE next-video experiment. If metrics are unavailable, state unavailable, not zero. Strong association in a small channel sample is not proof of causation.
